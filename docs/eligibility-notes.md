# Eligibility Notes

Common eligibility patterns across startup credit and grant programs. **Always confirm on the official program page** — these are patterns, not promises.

## The usual gates

- **Company age**: founded within the last 3–5 years is the most common bar (Anthropic's startup program: under 4 years; many cloud programs: under 5–10 years).
- **Funding stage**: pre-Series B / pre-Series C is typical for the largest tiers. Early tiers often accept pre-seed and bootstrapped companies; top tiers may require institutional backing.
- **Product stage**: a live product, public site, or at least a credible MVP. Pure idea-stage applications are usually rejected.
- **VC or accelerator affiliation**: some top tiers unlock only through partner VCs/accelerators (OpenAI startup credits are frequently partner-gated; AWS Activate's largest tier is accelerator-linked).
- **Not a prior recipient**: most programs are one-shot per company — you can't re-apply after using the credits.
- **Geography**: some grants are U.S.-only (Hello Alice, Freed Fellowship Grant); cloud programs are usually global.

## Credit mechanics to know

- **Expiry**: cloud credits almost always expire (commonly 12–24 months). Apply when you can spend, not speculatively.
- **Scope limits**: credits may exclude certain services (e.g. marketplace purchases, premium support, or specific regions).
- **Stacking**: holding credits from AWS, GCP, and Azure simultaneously is allowed; running production on all three is usually not worth it.
- **Tiered programs**: Cloudflare, Microsoft Founders Hub, and others use application-reviewed tiers — the headline number is the top tier, not the default.

## Grants vs accelerator funding

- **Non-dilutive grants** (Awesome Foundation, Amber Grant, FedEx Small Business Grant): free money, small amounts, competitive, no equity.
- **Accelerator investments** (Y Combinator, Techstars, PearX, Antler, 500 Global): larger checks in exchange for equity and a program commitment. Listed here because founders treat them as "runway packages", but they are investments, not credits — noted per entry.
